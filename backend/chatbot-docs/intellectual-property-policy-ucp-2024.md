# UCP Higher Education Intellectual Property for Students Policy 2024–2025

## 1. Introduction

Intellectual Property (IP) refers to creations of the mind—stories, inventions, artworks, etc.—that hold value and originality. This policy outlines how students can protect their IP created during their time at UCP, and what rights and responsibilities are involved.

Students retain ownership of IP they generate, but UCP may request assignment in special cases such as externally sponsored or collaborative work.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies exclusively to all Higher Education provision at University Centre Peterborough (UCP).

UCP complies with GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018. Student data is confidential but may be shared with UK authorities upon lawful request.

## 7. Procedure

### Ownership – General

- Students typically own the IP they create.
- UCP retains a royalty-free, irrevocable license to use the IP for educational, research, and promotional purposes.

### Externally Sponsored Work

- UCP may require students to assign IP rights when external funding or third-party resources are involved.
- Assignment arrangements should be established at the beginning of the project.

### Collaborative Work

- Students usually retain IP unless:
  - It is derived from UCP/staff-owned IP.
  - It was developed using additional UCP resources (beyond standard access).
- Students may need to grant a confirmatory assignment to UCP in such cases.

### Artistic Works

- Students retain copyright of their artistic works.
- UCP may use these works for educational and promotional purposes without royalties.
- Works will be properly credited.

## 8. Revenue Sharing

- Any commercial use of a student's work requires a license agreement.
- If UCP commercialises the IP, the student is entitled to a revenue share.

## 9. Disputes

Unresolvable disputes should be referred to the Accountable Officer or UCP Council. Complaints should be submitted using the [UCP complaints process](https://ucp.ac.uk/supporting-you/ucp-policies/).
